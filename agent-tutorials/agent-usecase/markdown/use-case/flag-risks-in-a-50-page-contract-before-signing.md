# Flag risks in a 50-page contract before signing

> The poster uploaded a 50-page contract and Claude flagged an auto-renewal near the end and a liability term they would have missed.

**Category:** Life Admin & Home  
**Agent:** Claude  
**Source:** Reddit · Jun 14, 2026

## What they did

The poster uploaded a 50-page contract PDF to Claude and used a prompt asking it to read the whole document, explain in plain English what they were agreeing to, list obligations and risks, say what a lawyer would flag as unusual or negotiable, and then name the three points to push back on hardest. Claude surfaced buried clauses, and the poster says a person should still bring a lawyer in for anything serious.

## How it went

It flagged three issues the poster says they would have missed, including an auto-renewal near the end and a liability term they say was not standard. The stated limitation is that it is a prep step, not a substitute for legal review.

## Worth knowing

The practical catch from the post is to use it to walk into a lawyer conversation knowing where the traps are, rather than relying on it alone.

## Prompt

```text
I'm about to sign the attached [type of contract, about N pages]. Read the whole thing and flag risks for me as [tenant / customer / freelancer / employee], especially auto-renewal clauses, liability and indemnity terms, termination and fees, and anything buried near the end. Give me a list of the issues with clause numbers, a plain-English explanation, and what I could ask to change. Note that this isn't legal advice.
```
