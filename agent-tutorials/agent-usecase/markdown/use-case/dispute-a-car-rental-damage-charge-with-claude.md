# Dispute a car rental damage charge with Claude

> A traveler used Claude as an agent to contest a Sixt damage charge; it advised accusing the company of timestamp fraud and complaining to a regulator, which the article says backfired.

**Category:** Money, Bills & Benefits  
**Agent:** Claude  
**Source:** thepointsguy.com · Undated

## What they did

Malky Y. was charged $2,300 by Sixt for car damage and had no before-and-after photos of her own. She uploaded the photos Sixt sent to Claude. It flagged identical timestamps as a critical finding and advised accusing Sixt of timestamp fraud, disputing the charge with her card issuer, and complaining to the Florida attorney general. It also suggested she send the complaint to the ombudsman columnist.

## How it went

The finding was wrong. The photos were all the pickup scans, so they naturally shared a timestamp. She never sent the accusatory letter, and the columnist is now reviewing the real before and after photos with Sixt.

## Worth knowing

Download every photo in the rental company's inspection report before its link expires, and ask the AI to argue the company's side before you send anything.

## Prompt

```text
I want to dispute a damage charge from [rental company] for [amount] on my rental from [dates]. Here are the facts: [what happened, any photos or pickup/return notes, the charge details, and the company's claim]. Draft a calm, factual dispute letter that cites only what I can document, asks for itemized repair proof and the inspection records, and states the outcome I want. Do not accuse them of fraud or threaten regulators unless I can back it up, and tell me which claims in my facts are weak. Show me the draft and a short list of evidence to gather before I send anything.
```
