# Run content creation with specialized bots

> Creators use bots to study outlier YouTube videos, and run newsletters with research, drafting, editing and scheduling bots; one experiment made 44 thumbnail proposals.

**Category:** Content & Creative  
**Agent:** Grok Bot  
**Source:** stealwhatworks.com · Undated

## What they did

Creators give each Grok Bot a persistent cloud computer and one narrow job. One YouTube Bot searches for videos running at least three times their channel's median, then studies topic, hook, format and thumbnail. A newsletter setup splits research, drafting, editing, scheduling and growth across Bots. In Ridark's seven-Bot team, each asset got its own group chat, and humans kept editing and publishing.

## How it went

In Ridark's first reported week, the seven Bots made 25 drafts and 14 were published after review. They made 44 thumbnail proposals and 15 were chosen. The article says there's no solid evidence yet of audience growth.

## Worth knowing

Bots hit expired logins, CAPTCHAs and site changes. All of one user's Bots share a single computer and its logins, so keep sensitive accounts off it.

## Prompt

```text
Act as my content team for [YouTube channel or newsletter on topic]. First research [5 outlier videos or top posts in my niche] and tell me what made them work, then draft [a script or issue] and an edit pass based on that. Give me a proposed schedule and don't publish or send anything until I approve it.
```
